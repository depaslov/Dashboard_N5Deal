N5Deal — September 2026 Content and Task Plan

Prepared by Alina · 31 August 2026 · Execution plan, not a proposal

How to read this

This is the single plan. It merges the Q4 OKRs, the website conversion audit, the content series proposal and the design-system campaign into one calendar, so that no channel is planned in isolation and no OKR counter is left to chance.

Two numbers govern it. Thirty-one pieces are produced across September, against a ceiling of six to eight per week. Those thirty-one pieces make thirty-eight placements, because a carousel built once appears on LinkedIn and Instagram, and a newsletter argument runs again as an X article. Production is the constraint; placement is nearly free. Every efficiency in this plan comes from that distinction.

Founder inputs appear below as dated requests with escalation dates, not as open questions. Where an input does not arrive, the fallback is stated.

The four core messages

O4 KR1 asks for four messages agreed with the founders. These are proposed for sign-off by Friday 4 September; the calendar runs on them from Tuesday 1 either way, because publishing nothing while waiting is the worse outcome. Each is evidenced from the audit rather than invented.

  • M1 — What you can buy today. Priced, comparable, live inventory. Tetra, Eternity Law and Ifbusiness all gate price behind an enquiry form; our licence pages publish regulator, permitted activities, timeline, share capital and an indicative price. This is the one advantage we should never trade away.
  • M2 — What a buyer actually checks. Trust as a conversion prerequisite. The colour audit, the Validated badge, the verification block, the proof layer. The buyer’s dominant private question is whether they are about to be defrauded, and we currently answer it nowhere.
  • M3 — Where the market is moving. Deal intelligence with sources and dates attached. The defensible line is that deal count is up, headline value is down, and activity has moved to assets our buyers can afford. We never say fintech M&A is booming flat.
  • M4 — Selling a licensed entity. Sell-side and partner content, led by confidentiality. Serves O5, where partners are the highest-leverage of the three sides and the channel is uncontested by all three benchmark competitors.

Risks

  • Approval queue, not capacity, is the binding constraint. Weeks 3 and 4 are gated on a single approval from Ihor by Friday 11 September. Mitigation: the listings engine backfills both weeks from templated inventory content, which needs no approval, at the cost of the Series A launch slipping to October.
  • Roman’s Tier 1 fixes underpin the trust posts. Publishing M2 content about governance discipline while the site shows three different jurisdiction counts and alt text reading “N5Bank Logo” is a contradiction our own audience is paid to notice. Mitigation: brief issued 1 September, escalate with screenshots on 8 September.
  • Two published-output posts and the entire O2 micro-purchase counter rest on one product going live. Mitigation: if the KYF report slips past 11 September, both slots convert to listings-engine posts and the O2 counter is formally reported as blocked rather than missed.
